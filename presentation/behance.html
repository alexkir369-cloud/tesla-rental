Goals

The brief was collected from the client's requests and turned into a short, measurable set of goals.

01

Sell without phone calls

Let a customer pick a model, see the exact price for their dates and send a booking request online, 24/7.

02

Transparent pricing

Euro prices with 19% VAT included, deposit and discounts visible before the request is sent — no hidden charges.

03

Premium dark brand

A calm, cinematic look that makes seven electric cars feel like a fleet worth flying for.

04

Easy for the owner to update

Plain files: prices and copy live in readable arrays and HTML — editable without a developer or a build pipeline.

Booking requests

Primary conversion — a submitted request with dates and contacts.

Mobile share

Scroll to rates

How many visitors reach the price table — the clearest sign the offer is understood.

Time to request

Landing → submitted form. Target: under two minutes with the calculator.

Technical brief

Audience & scenarios

Business traveller

Lands in Berlin, needs a car in an hour

Books a Model S Plaid or Model X for airport pick-up, expects an invoice with VAT and a named driver on the policy. Cares about status, speed and zero paperwork.

Family & tourists

Long weekend, 500 km of countryside

Takes a Model Y or the 7-seat version. Needs range confidence, child seat, unlimited mileage and a clear answer on where to charge.

Creators & events

Shoot on Saturday, wedding in June

Rents a Cybertruck or Model X with Falcon Wing doors for a few hours. Needs a car that photographs well and an operator who delivers on time.

What it means for the UI

  • Prices and deposits are always visible, also in the fleet cards.
  • Airport delivery and city delivery are separate options with fixed prices.
  • Range, seats and drivetrain are shown as scannable chips, not paragraphs.

Language & market

  • English copy, EUR prices, EU/EEA licence rules and 19% VAT.
  • Cities: Berlin, Munich, Amsterdam; airports BER, MUC, AMS.
  • Cross-border travel inside the EU is allowed and explained in the FAQ.

02 · Brief05 / 17
Technical brief

Non-functional requirements & constraints

Responsive 2560 → 390

Fluid container 1200 → 1320 → 1480 px, car cards and galleries reflow, hero collapses to one column below 900 px.

No backend

The site is static; the booking form validates and stores the request in the browser, structured so a CRM or Telegram bot can be plugged into one handler.

Licence-clean media only

Every photo and clip comes from Wikimedia Commons or Mixkit with a documented licence in credits.json.

Accessibility

Semantic markup, visible :focus-visible rings, keyboard-friendly accordion, and all motion disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.

Performance budget

Lazy media, 800 px thumbnails via srcset, hero preload, video preload="none" with posters to avoid layout shift.

Maintainability

One CSS design system, one JS file with data arrays (models, extras, discounts), one README with a replacement checklist.

No CMS

Seven models and six extras live in one readable array; the owner can change a price in a text editor and reload the page.

No tracking scripts

Nothing third-party is loaded: only Google Fonts for Inter, with a system fallback. Analytics can be added deliberately later.

No fake urgency

No countdown timers or “2 cars left” pressure — the only live number is the genuine availability card in the hero.

Rules

04 · Design system09 / 17
Key solution 01

Cars without background

Studio-style cards instead of stock photos: every model is cut out from a real photo and placed on a dark panel with a blue shadow and glow.

1 · Find a whole car

The first attempt failed: several source photos were tight crops, so the cut-out inherited a sliced bumper. Edge coverage was measured to catch this automatically (0.72 → 0.07).

2 · Cut with Apple Vision

A small Swift tool runs VNGenerateForegroundInstanceMaskRequest offline — no third-party model, no upload, transparent WebP out (75–110 KB each).

3 · Light it

A blurred blue ellipse plus a two-stop drop-shadow anchors the car; on hover the shadow deepens and the car lifts 4 px.

05 · Solutions10 / 17
Key solution 02

Motion that carries the fleet

A continuous strip of all seven models right under the hero, plus a spec ticker, counted-up statistics and pointer-aware highlights.

Car marquee

14 tiles (7 + a duplicate) scroll seamlessly, pause on hover, each tile links to its fleet card.

Spec ticker

Range, acceleration, deposit and delivery scroll as a thin band and pause on hover.

Count-up stats

Fleet size and delivery time animate once, when the hero enters the viewport.

Respect the user

Every animation is GPU-only (transform/opacity) and switched off under reduced-motion.

05 · Solutions11 / 17
Key solution 03

Video & media

Three short clips show what the cars actually do: a country road, an autumn forest and an aerial highway view.

Behaviour

Muted, looping, playsinline. Starts only when the block is 35% visible and pauses when it leaves the screen.

Weight

preload="none" plus a poster frame per clip: 2.7 MB, 4.2 MB and 4.8 MB, nothing loads until it is needed.

Honesty about sourcing

Free-licensed stock footage is credited in credits.json. A composite “Tesla driving” clip was prototyped and then dropped: it did not look real enough.

05 · Solutions12 / 17
Key solution 04

Layout that scales

From a 2560 px desktop to a 390 px phone — the same components, re-composed. The container grows with the screen instead of freezing at 1200 px.

2560 px

Container 1480 px, 3-column grids, hero 21:9 media band, four-column footer.

1440 px

Container 1200 px, two-column hero with a floating cut-out car and quote card.

1024 px

Filters wrap, tables become scrollable, page headers keep a photo backdrop.

390 px

Single column, car above the headline, compact header, marquee still full-width.

  • Fluid container: 1200 → 1320 (≥1500) → 1480 px (≥1900).
  • Images: 800 px thumbnails in srcset for phones, 1600 px for desktop.
  • Video: 21:9 on desktop, 16:9 on phones, always full-bleed.
